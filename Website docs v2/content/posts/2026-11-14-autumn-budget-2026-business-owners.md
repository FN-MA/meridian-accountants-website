**Quick answer:** the Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026 left corporation tax, dividend tax and National Insurance unchanged, so a small salary plus dividends is still the usual mix for company directors. The big changes are to **capital allowances**: the first-year allowance on new plant and machinery rises from 40% to **50% from 1 January 2027**, the main pool writing down allowance falls from 14% to **12% from April 2027**, and the **100% allowance for new electric company cars ends on 31 March 2027**.

If you run a limited company or your own business, here's what the Budget means for you, with dates to plan around and a worked example of salary versus dividends for 2026/27.

## Capital allowances: what's changing

Capital allowances are how you get tax relief when your business buys equipment, machinery, vans and other assets. The Budget changed two rates.

| Allowance | Now | From | New rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First-year allowance on new main-rate plant and machinery | 40% | 1 January 2027 | **50%** |
| Main pool writing down allowance (each year on the remaining balance) | 14% | 1 April 2027 (companies), 6 April 2027 (sole traders and partnerships) | **12%** |
| Annual Investment Allowance | £1 million | No change | £1 million |
| Full expensing for companies (new main-rate plant) | 100% | No change | 100% |
| Special rate pool writing down allowance | 6% | No change | 6% |

**What this means in practice:**

- **Most small businesses won't notice the first-year allowance change.** The **Annual Investment Allowance** already gives 100% relief on up to £1 million a year of qualifying equipment, for companies, sole traders and partnerships alike. The 40% (rising to 50%) first-year allowance matters mainly for spending above £1 million, or for companies buying assets to lease out, which full expensing doesn't cover.
- **The writing down allowance cut affects everyone with a balance in the main pool**, including assets bought years ago. Relief on the remaining balance slows from 14% to 12% a year.
- **Cars don't qualify** for the Annual Investment Allowance, full expensing or the new 50% rate. That's why the electric car change below matters so much.
- **Second-hand assets** don't qualify for first-year allowances, but they can still use the Annual Investment Allowance.

If your accounting year straddles 1 April 2027, a blended rate applies for that year.

## Electric company cars: the 100% allowance ends

Until now, a company buying a **new, zero-emission car** could claim a **100% first-year allowance**, deducting the full cost from profits in the year of purchase. That ends:

- **31 March 2027** for companies
- **5 April 2027** for sole traders and partnerships

After that, new electric cars go into the main pool at the new **12%** writing down allowance. They don't qualify for the 50% first-year allowance.

**Example:** a company buys a £50,000 electric car.

|  | Bought by 31 March 2027 | Bought from 1 April 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Allowance in the first year | £50,000 (100%) | £6,000 (12%) |
| Corporation tax saved in the first year, at 25% | £12,500 | £1,500 |

The rest of the relief still comes, but slowly, over many years. **The car must be delivered, not just ordered, by the deadline**, and lead times can be long. If you're thinking about an electric company car, start the conversation now.

**Benefit in kind for the driver.** An electric company car is still very tax-efficient for the driver. The benefit-in-kind rate rises gradually:

| Tax year | Benefit-in-kind rate for electric cars |
| --- | --- |
| 2026/27 | 4% |
| 2027/28 | 5% |
| 2028/29 | 7% |
| 2029/30 | 9% |
| 2030/31 | 10% |

On a £50,000 car in 2027/28, that's a taxable benefit of £2,500, or £1,000 of tax a year for a higher-rate taxpayer.

**Other car changes:**

- **From April 2027**, company car benefits must be reported through payroll each month instead of on a P11D.
- **From April 2028**, a pay-per-mile charge on electric cars is due to start, at 3p a mile (1.5p for plug-in hybrids).
- **Business mileage in your own car:** the approved rate stays at 55p for the first 10,000 miles, and the rate above 10,000 miles rises from 25p to **35p from 6 April 2027**.

## Salary vs dividends: no change, so the usual approach still works

The Chancellor didn't change income tax, National Insurance, dividend tax or corporation tax rates. The rates for 2026/27 carry on into 2027/28:

|  | 2026/27 and 2027/28 |
| --- | --- |
| Personal allowance | £12,570 (frozen) |
| Basic rate band | Up to £50,270 |
| Dividend allowance | £500 |
| Dividend tax rates | 10.75% basic, 35.75% higher, 39.35% additional |
| Employer National Insurance | 15% on salary above £5,000 a year |
| Employment Allowance | £10,500 (not available if the only employee is a director) |
| Corporation tax | 19% up to £50,000 profit, 25% above £250,000, marginal relief in between |

### Worked example: a one-person company

A director is the company's only employee, has no other income and wants to take out all of the year's profit of **£60,000**. A common approach is a salary of £12,570, with the rest as dividends.

|  | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Profit before salary | £60,000 |
| Salary | £12,570 |
| Employer National Insurance (15% of £7,570) | £1,136 |
| Profit after salary and NI | £46,294 |
| Corporation tax at 19% | £8,796 |
| Available for dividends | £37,498 |
| Dividend tax (first £500 free, then 10.75%) | £3,977 |
| **Total tax and NI** | **£13,909** |
| **Director takes home** | **£46,091** |

The salary uses up the personal allowance, so no income tax or employee National Insurance is due on it, and it counts towards the State Pension. The employer National Insurance is deductible for corporation tax, which is why £12,570 usually still beats a £5,000 salary for a one-person company, by around £500 a year here.

If the company has another employee and can claim the Employment Allowance, there's no employer National Insurance on the director's salary at all.

### Other ways to take money out

- **Employer pension contributions** remain one of the most tax-efficient routes: deductible for corporation tax, and no income tax or National Insurance.
- **Repaying your director's loan account** if you've lent the company money. See our [director's loan account guide](https://meridian-accountants.com/blog/directors-loan-account-explained/).
- **Leaving profit in the company** if you don't need it all now, to be taken in a lower-income year.

**One change to watch:** from **6 April 2027**, tax on **savings and property income** rises by 4 percentage points, to 24%, 44% and 49%. Dividend rates aren't affected, but if you hold rental property or large savings personally, it's worth reviewing.

## Other Budget changes worth knowing

| Change | From |
| --- | --- |
| Trading allowance rises from £1,000 to **£3,000**, so more side-income can go untaxed and unreported | 6 April 2027 |
| National Living Wage rises to **£13.21** an hour for workers aged 21 and over | 1 April 2027 |
| Company cars, vans, fuel and medical benefits reported through **payroll** instead of P11Ds | April 2027 |
| Business Asset Disposal Relief lifetime limit cut from £1 million to **£500,000** (rate stays 18%) | 6 April 2027 |
| New **Founders' Relief**: 10% capital gains tax on up to £10 million of gains for qualifying founders (conditions still being consulted on) | 6 April 2027 |
| Business rates relief in England for retail, hospitality and leisure properties: **100%** relief up to £18,000 rateable value | 1 April 2027 |
| VAT registration threshold stays at **£90,000** | No change |

Most of these changes still need to pass through the Finance Bill, so details could change before they take effect.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does the 50% first-year allowance start?

It applies to spending on new main-rate plant and machinery on or after 1 January 2027. Cars don't qualify. Most small businesses will still use the £1 million Annual Investment Allowance, which gives 100% relief.

### Does the cut to 12% affect equipment I've already bought?

Yes. From April 2027, the writing down allowance on the balance in your main pool falls from 14% to 12%, so tax relief on older purchases comes through more slowly.

### Should my company buy an electric car before April 2027?

If you were already planning to, buying before 1 April 2027 (6 April for sole traders and partnerships) means you can claim 100% of the cost in one go. The car must be new, unused and delivered by then, not just ordered. After that, relief drops to 12% a year.

### Is a £12,570 salary still the best option for a director?

For a one-person company that can't claim the Employment Allowance, £12,570 is usually still the most tax-efficient salary, with the rest taken as dividends. The right mix depends on your profit, other income and pension plans.

### Are dividend tax rates going up in April 2027?

No. Dividend rates stay at 10.75%, 35.75% and 39.35%, with a £500 allowance. The rise to 24%, 44% and 49% applies to savings and property income.

## In short

- The first-year allowance on new main-rate equipment rises to 50% from 1 January 2027, but the main pool writing down allowance falls to 12% from April 2027.
- The 100% allowance for new electric company cars ends in April 2027. If you're planning to buy one, the timing matters.
- Salary and dividend rates don't change, so a £12,570 salary plus dividends still works for most one-person companies.
